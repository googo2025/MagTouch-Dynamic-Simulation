Model-specific contribution and conclusion

We position flexible magnetic-tactile simulation as a research data environment for discovering and evaluating useful model-improvement directions, not as a requirement that every candidate improves every motion. The source-fitted, symmetrically axis-separated dynamic surrogate supports continuous interaction, diverse motion generation and causal reconstruction loops. The sufficiently trained 1,000-trajectory augmentation candidate reduces paired XY error on previously recorded, held-out Yellow trials from 0.740 to 0.630 mm (14.8%). In two separately acquired online batches following the same new-motion protocol, the first complex path improves translation-removed XY error from 0.267 to 0.189 mm (29.4%) and reduces adjacent residual-jump rate from 3.91% to 1.90% (51.4%). The second path also reduces jumps by 33.0%, while its translation-removed error increases. A distinct ordinary 128-trajectory candidate reduces jumps on all 36 complex real trial records across three batches, with batch-average reductions of 14.5%, 14.1% and 6.2%, and lower dwell fluctuation. These are separate, model-specific contributions: target-device transfer on existing recordings, motion-dependent joint improvement in trajectory shape and continuity, and improved temporal stability. Absolute-coordinate bias and accuracy costs remain reported; retrospective translation removal is not an online origin calibration. Simulation therefore provides reproducible direction discovery and conditional gains rather than universal optimality or a validated full-physics digital twin.
